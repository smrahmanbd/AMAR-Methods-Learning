1. A model finds that roads with more police patrols have more crashes. Patrols are assigned to high-crash roads.

2. The same model, estimated on 2015–2018 data and again on 2021–2024 data, gives a different sign for “night-time”.

3. Motorcycle helmets reduce injury severity on average, yet the benefit seems to differ widely between riders in ways the crash report cannot explain.

4. A deep-learning model forecasts next month’s crash counts very accurately, but cannot say what a lower speed limit would do.

5. A study has no crashes at a new junction design, so it measures time-to-collision from video for two weeks.

6. Ten consecutive segments of one motorway all have more crashes than the model expects.

7. A random-parameters model cannot be solved exactly, so the software uses 1,000 Halton draws per observation.

8. Drivers who choose cars with advanced safety systems may already be more careful than average.
