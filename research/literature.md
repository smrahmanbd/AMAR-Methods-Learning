## Recent papers

| Idea | Paper | In plain words |
|---|---|---|
| prediction, marginal effects, temporal tests | Hou, Q., Huo, X., Leng, J. and Mannering, F. (2022). A note on out-of-sample prediction, marginal effects computations, and temporal testing with random parameters crash-injury severity models. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 33, 100191. | Compared a fixed-parameter multinomial logit with three random-parameter versions. The version with heterogeneity in means and variances predicted best out of sample. Two ways of computing marginal effects both gave defensible results. Testing years pair by pair revealed more detail than one global test. |
| temporal instability | Shabab, K. R., Bhowmik, T., Zaki, M. H. and Eluru, N. (2024). A systematic unified approach for addressing temporal instability in road safety analysis. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research*, 100335. | Proposes one unified framework for handling temporal instability, in place of estimating a separate model for every period. (Only the title and citation could be checked for this site; read the paper for the method.) |
| crash–conflict relationship | Chen, Z., Yastremska-Kravchenko, O., Laureshyn, A., Johnsson, C. and D’Agostino, C. (2024). Stochastic method based on copulas for predicting severe road traffic interactions. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 44, 100347. | Combines copulas (a way to describe how two measures move together) with extreme value theory, so that interactions of all severities are used, not only the most extreme. Crash-probability estimates were similar to the usual two-measure threshold method, and it handled events the older method could not define. |
| crash–conflict relationship | Niu, D., Sayed, T., Fu, C. and Mannering, F. (2024). A cross-comparison of different extreme value modeling techniques for traffic conflict-based crash risk estimation. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 44, 100352. | Compares several extreme value approaches for turning conflicts into crash estimates. A threshold that adapts to conditions fitted better than a fixed one, and using two conflict measures together with an adaptive threshold predicted best overall. |
| crash–conflict relationship | Nazir, F., Ali, Y. and Haque, M. M. (2024). Effects of sample size on pedestrian crash risk estimation from traffic conflicts using extreme value models. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 44, 100353. | Asks how much conflict data is enough. Estimates improved with more data, block-maxima models were more sensitive to sample size, and peak-over-threshold models were more stable. Too small a sample gives unreliable crash estimates with wide uncertainty. |
| prediction versus causality | Seyfi, M. A., Karimi Mamaghan, A. M., Behnood, A. and Mannering, F. (2025). Analyzing crash injury severities with deep learning and advanced statistical models: An assessment of methodological challenges. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 48, 100405. | Compares deep neural networks with random parameters logit models on motorcycle crash severity. The effects of variables implied by deep learning often disagreed with the statistical models in size and sometimes in direction, and shifts over time were hard to handle in deep learning. |
| unobserved heterogeneity, temporal instability | Alnawmasi, N. and Mannering, F. (2025). A note on random parameters models of crash injury severities with k-means clustering for data preprocessing. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 48, 100408. | First groups similar crashes with a simple clustering method, then fits random parameters models with heterogeneity in means and variances to each cluster and year. Likelihood ratio tests supported the clusters, and the clustered models fitted better and gave more precise marginal effects. |
| temporal instability, latent classes | Bhowmik, T., Pervaz, S., Mannering, F. and Eluru, N. (2026). An exploratory latent segmentation approach to account for temporally shifting parameters in driver injury-severity models. *Analytic Methods in Accident Research* 50, 100428. | Lets the model sort crashes into hidden segments and asks which segment changes over time. Using three survey years, change was concentrated in a low-injury-risk segment, while the high-risk segment stayed stable. |
